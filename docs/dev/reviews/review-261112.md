# Whole-project audit — 2026-11-12 (main 59fa4b4, v0.26.0)

The core solvers agree with nec2c on well-formed decks. Most findings sit at the edges: how the parser reads a card compared with nec2c, shared-seam rules that exist in two copies, and a few unbounded resources. Two are high severity: `--ground-solver sommerfeld` returns a feedpoint reactance 2× to 500× wrong, with no warning, for a wire lower than about half a segment length above ground, and the low-ground warning names that flag as the remedy (A1); and the distributed controller never matches a result to its task, so one stray stdout line from a worker shifts every later impedance onto the next frequency (A2).

## Method

- **Finders:** nine Opus finders, one per dimension, at most five findings each:
  1. parser-positional
  2. excitation-sources
  3. frontend-parity
  4. panics-and-hostile-input
  5. test-integrity
  6. gpu-and-concurrency
  7. solver-correctness
  8. docs-and-tooling
  9. defect-archetypes
- **Verifiers:** each finding went to its own verifier with a default verdict of *refuted*. A finding was kept only when the verifier reproduced it, with the prebuilt release `fnec` at 59fa4b4 and nec2c 1.3.1 (and, where named, NEC-2D or 4nec2's `nec2dxs1K5.exe` under Wine). Verifiers re-rated severity where the evidence called for it, and corrected framing and remedies; those corrections are kept in the ledger rows.
- **Synthesis:** on a different model (Sonnet). Findings that describe one defect from two dimensions were merged.

## Result

- **35 candidates** from the nine finders: 5 each from parser-positional, excitation-sources, frontend-parity and docs-and-tooling; 4 from test-integrity; 3 each from panics-and-hostile-input, gpu-and-concurrency and defect-archetypes; 2 from solver-correctness.
- **34 confirmed, 1 refuted.** The refuted one (test-integrity) said the GC fixtures are all centre-fed and mirror-symmetric, so a RAD1/RAD2 swap or an inverted RDEL would pass. The fixture observation is right for `apps/nec-cli/tests/gc_card_nec2c.rs`, but `a_gc_taper_matches_nec2cs_segmentation_table` (`crates/nec_solver/src/geometry.rs`, tests) parses an asymmetric slanted `GW`/`GC 0 0 1.2 .1 .3` through the real parse and build path and asserts every segment's midpoint, length and radius against nec2c's segmentation table to 5.1e-5. A swap or inversion fails it by about 0.2 in radius and 0.37 m in length.
- **29 entries after merges** (A1–A29). Five entries each merge two confirmed findings: A3, A4, A10, A12 and A26.
- **2 high, 9 medium, 18 low.**

The synthesized report's summary says "all 32 surviving findings"; the verified set holds 34 confirmed findings, and 29 entries plus 5 merges account for all 34.

## Classes and standing checks

Classes with two or more findings need a standing check.

1. **Parser reads a card differently from nec2c (A3, A4, A5, A13, A14, A21).** The largest class; four of these decks give a silently different antenna at exit 0 on every frontend, because the parser is shared.
   - Standing check: a differential corpus of malformed and edge-case cards (blank or zero fields, indented lines, fractions, negatives, split groups), run through nec2c and fnec, requiring an identical answer or a named refusal. Fold it into the FIELD_DISPOSITION tables, which today probe only non-zero values.
2. **"Same tag" treated as "same wire" (A11, A18, A19).** A grep for `.tag ==` pair skips found two sites (`geometry.rs:1804` and `validate.rs:112`). A11 comes from the same belief upstream, in wire-boundary detection.
   - Standing check: a renumbering metamorphic test. Re-tag the decks (all distinct, all 1, all 0) and require the same answer, caveats and refusals. Better, carry a wire or card id on `Segment`.
3. **One integer or field rule, several readers (A13, A21).** The `parse_whole` rule exists but is bypassed in at least three places (GM ITGI, `parse_u32`, the ignored-card path).
   - Standing check: a lint or test that forbids `as u32` and `as i32` on a parsed field outside the shared reader.
4. **Multi-source decks not handled at some seam (A6, A12, A3 for EX).** FND-291, FND-304 and FND-308 fixed three frontends; the GUI and the caveat text remain.
   - Standing check: a two-source phased-array deck in the parity suite across every frontend, asserting every feed is visible and no caveat is false.
5. **Warning or caveat dropped on one route (A17, A20, A15, A10).** The same deck gets a different caveat set depending on solver mode, route or frontend.
   - Standing check: route-parity tests that run the caveat-triggering decks through each path (main, converge, sweep, GUI, worker) and compare the warning sets.
6. **A refusal or caveat is false or names a wrong remedy (A1, A6, A11, A22, A4).** Sommerfeld named as the fix, "physically impossible" on a real array, "no adapter" for a lost device.
   - Standing check: every caveat remedy is exercised by a test that follows it and verifies the result actually improves.
7. **Unbounded resource at an aggregate or at the other end of a protocol (A7, A8, A16).** The per-item caps exist, but the product, the empty loop and the read side were missed.
   - Standing check: the adversarial-input checklist per protocol end and per sweep dimension, with a bounded-time and bounded-memory test for each.
8. **Gates that cannot fail (A9, A28, A27, A29).** A monotone-only check, a one-spelling grep, a loop-fed pass and a symmetric fixture.
   - Standing check: a committed known-fail input for every gate, plus an absolute nec2c band wherever nec2c is cited.
9. **Documentation drift and splices (A15, A24, A25, A26).**
   - Standing check: a docs gate for fence balance, table-header presence, and status lines checked against the code.

Single-finding areas: A1 (Sommerfeld low height), A2 (worker desync), A23 (LD 5 reference limit) and A10's auto-pick seam. A1 and A2 are the two that most need attention first.
